How it works

The two ideas

Carryover. An ad keeps working after the week it ran. The stock for week t is that week's spend plus a share of last week's stock.

Saturation. The first dollars do more than the later ones. Lift is a Hill curve of the stock, so it rises quickly, then flattens.

stock(t) = spend(t) + carryover x stock(t - 1) lift(t) = max x stock(t)^a / (stock(t)^a + half^a) half-life = ln(0.5) / ln(carryover) weeks
Why this matters for modelling

Marketing mix models estimate these two shapes for each channel from your sales history. A model that ignores carryover blames the wrong week. One that ignores saturation says to put everything in the best channel. Seeing the shapes here makes the model output easier to question.

Read this before you use a number

This is a toy, not a model. Nothing here is estimated from data. The numbers show how the shapes behave and are not benchmarks for any channel.

Real carryover and saturation are unknown. They have to be estimated and tested, and different data can give different answers.

The curve shapes are modelling choices. Geometric carryover and a Hill curve are common. Real behaviour can differ.
